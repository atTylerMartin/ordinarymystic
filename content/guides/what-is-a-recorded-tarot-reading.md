A recorded tarot reading is a reading I prepare privately, off camera, for your question. Within three business days of receiving your question, you get two things by email: a private YouTube link to a video walkthrough of the spread, and a written synthesis you can keep. You watch it when you have the attention for it.

That is the short version. The rest of this guide is about how it works, why it is built the way it is, and who it suits. If you already know you want one, the [recorded readings page](/readings/recorded) has the lengths and the booking buttons.

## What arrives, and when?

Two things, in one email, within three business days of my having your question.

The first is a private YouTube link. It is not posted on a channel or listed anywhere; it is a link to a video made for you. In it you see the spread on the table, and I talk you through what the cards are saying about your question, card by card and then as a whole.

The second is a written synthesis. It is the reading boiled down to what matters: the main thread, the tension the cards point at, and the few things worth acting on or watching for.

The length you book sets how long the walkthrough runs. Fifteen minutes is right for one question looked at closely. Thirty gives a question room to be seen from several angles, or covers two related ones. Sixty is for several questions and the connections between them.

## How does your question reach me?

At checkout. The Stripe form asks for your question in a sentence, and that one sentence is enough for me to start.

If there is background you want me to have, you can send it by replying to your Stripe receipt, or by email. What is going on, what you have already tried, and what prompted the question all help. None of it is required.

If something in the question is unclear, I ask by email before I read. I would rather spend one short email getting the question right than spend a whole reading answering the wrong one. The three business days start once I have the question.

Most people find the question is the hardest part. If you are unsure how to phrase yours, [Asking a Question the Cards Can Answer](/guides/asking-a-question-the-cards-can-answer) walks through it.

## How do I prepare a recorded reading?

I read your question before I touch the cards, usually more than once. A question about whether to take a job and a question about why you keep doubting yourself at work can sound alike and need very different readings, so I want to know which one I am holding.

Then I lay out the spread privately, with no camera on, and sit with it. I look at each card, but mostly I am looking at what the cards are doing together: where they agree, where they pull against each other, what keeps coming up. I make notes as I go.

Only then do I record. So the video is not me reacting to cards for the first time. It is me explaining a reading I have already worked through. The written synthesis comes last, once I have seen the whole thing.

That order is the main reason the format exists. Nobody is waiting on me while I think, so the thinking gets the time it needs.

## What does the video do that the written synthesis doesn't?

They do different jobs, which is why you get both.

The video shows you the reading. You see the cards, where they fall in the spread, and how one card changes what the next one means. You hear the reasoning, not only the conclusion. If you have ever had a reading where someone told you what a card meant and you could not follow how they got there, the video is the fix for that.

The synthesis is the part you keep. A week or a month later, when the situation has moved, you are not going to rewatch a thirty-minute video to find the one point you half remember. You are going to open a page and read it in two minutes. The synthesis is written for that moment.

Some people watch the video once and live with the synthesis. Some rewatch the video every few weeks. Both are fine. It is yours.

## Who is a recorded reading for?

People who want time with it. If you take things in better when you can pause, replay and come back, a recorded reading fits how you already work.

People who want a considered answer more than a fast one. Because I prepare the reading without anyone waiting, it gets slower attention than a reading done on the spot.

People whose schedules do not line up with anyone else's. There is no appointment to find. You book when the question occurs to you, and the reading arrives when it is ready.

People who are a little nervous about a reading. Some people would rather not be on camera, or would rather hear something hard on their own, in their own time, than across a screen from a stranger.

People who want to come back to it. A reading about a decision often makes more sense after the decision than before, and having it on record means you can check.

## When should you book a live reading instead?

When the conversation is the point. A [live reading](/readings/live) is one on one over Zoom: the cards come out while you watch, you hear my thinking as it happens, and you can interrupt, add context, or chase a thread I would not have known to follow. The reading changes direction because of what you say. A written synthesis follows afterward.

If you know your question is tangled, and you expect to need to explain things as they come up, live will serve you better. The same goes if you simply prefer talking to watching.

Neither format is the lesser one. Recorded buys preparation time and something you can keep. Live buys real-time access and back and forth, which is why it costs more for the same length of time. If you are weighing the two, [Recorded, Live, or Written: Which Reading to Book](/guides/recorded-live-or-written-tarot-reading) lays them side by side.

## What is a recorded reading not?

It is not a generic video. A lot of what turns up under "pre-recorded tarot reading" is general content made for anyone who clicks: pick a pile, and the reading applies to whoever chose it. That can be fun. It is not this. A recorded reading here is made for one person and one question, and nobody else sees it.

It is not a template. I do not have a stock reading for the Tower that gets pasted in. The same card means different things next to different cards and under different questions, and the walkthrough is built around yours.

It is not a per-minute call. The large psychic platforms, Keen and California Psychics among them, price readings by the minute, which puts a clock on the conversation. A recorded reading has a set length and a set price, agreed before I start.

It is not a prediction. I do not tell you what will happen. I look at timing, tendencies and patterns, and you always keep the choice. A good reading leaves you with a clearer view of your situation and a few concrete things to try, not a verdict.

It is not a psychic reading. I am not a psychic or a medium. I read the cards as a structured way to look at a question you already have, from angles you would not reach alone.

It is not just an email. "Tarot reading by email" can mean anything from a paragraph to a photo of three cards. For what it's worth, Etsy, where a lot of readings are sold, requires a reading listed there to come with something tangible, such as photos of the spread, audio or video, or the text of the reading. A recorded reading here is the video and the written synthesis together, every time.
