A good online tarot reader tells you the price before you pay, says what you will receive and when, is a named person with a history you can check, and promises nothing about outcomes. Walk away from anyone who says you are cursed, finds a new fee after every reading, or tells you to decide today.

I'm Tyler Martin, and I read tarot online, so I have a stake in this. Read what follows as a checklist you can hold me to as well as anyone else. Where it helps, I say how I handle each point, so you have one concrete example to compare against.

## What does a sound tarot practice look like from the outside?

You can learn most of what you need before you book. A practice that is run with care tends to show it in plain, checkable ways.

**The price is public.** You should be able to see what a reading costs before you hand over a card number, and the price you see should be the price you pay. Per-minute billing is not a scam in itself, but you should know the rate and how the meter runs. Surprise charges are an old problem in this field: the FTC took a psychic hotline operation to court in 2002 over costs misrepresented in its advertising and bills for services nobody bought, and the Iowa Attorney General was still warning about unexpectedly high hotline charges years later. My prices sit on the [recorded](/readings/recorded) and [live](/readings/live) pages, and checkout charges exactly that. [What an online tarot reading costs](/guides/online-tarot-reading-cost) goes into what is normal.

**The turnaround and the delivery are stated.** For a recorded reading, you should know what arrives, how, and by when. Mine arrives by email within three business days of my receiving your question: a private YouTube link to a video walkthrough, plus a written synthesis. For a live reading, you should know how a time gets set and where you will meet. Mine are one on one over Zoom, and I email within two business days of booking to schedule. If a reader can't tell you this up front, you will be chasing it later.

**A named person with a history.** You should be able to find out who is actually reading for you: a name, how long they have been at it, how they work. A stage name is common and not a problem on its own. What matters is that the person behind it is consistent and findable over time. My [about page](/about) says who I am, where I live and how long I have been reading.

**Reviews you can check.** Look for reviews on the reader's own site or on a third-party profile, and read a few in full. Specifics are worth more than stars: what the reading helped someone see, what they did next. A practice with no reviews yet isn't automatically suspect, since everyone starts somewhere, but one that gets cagey when you ask is.

**No promises about outcomes.** A reader who guarantees that your ex comes back, the money arrives or the diagnosis changes is selling something the cards can't deliver. AARP lists exactly this, promising outcomes beyond the reader's control, as a warning sign. I don't predict the future. A reading with me looks at timing, tendencies and what is worth paying attention to, and you keep the choice.

**Clear limits on health, legal and money questions.** Tarot can help you think about a medical decision, a contract or a job offer. It can't stand in for the doctor, the lawyer or the accountant. A sound reader says so without being asked. My [terms](/terms) put it plainly: a reading is not medical, legal or financial advice.

## What are the red flags of a tarot or psychic scam?

The red flags have almost nothing to do with the cards. They are sales tactics, and consumer-protection offices describe the same handful again and again.

**You are told you are cursed.** This is the classic opening. The reader finds a curse, a block or some darkness around you, and offers to remove it for a fee. The removal never quite works, so there is another ceremony, and then another. A 2023 news release from the Florida Attorney General describes a so-called psychic who approached an upset woman in a mall, told her she was cursed, named a price to lift it, and kept pressing her for more over the following weeks. The case ended with a prison sentence of nearly ten years. A tarot reading has nothing to remove. If a reader says otherwise, the reading is over.

**The fees keep growing.** One reading turns into a cleansing, a ritual, candles, a follow-up you didn't ask for. AARP's version of this warning sign is a psychic who keeps finding new things wrong with you or your life, each with a fee attached. A sound practice sells a reading at a stated price and stops there.

**Urgency.** You have to decide today. The window closes tonight. Something bad will happen to you, or to someone you love, if you don't pay now. Pressure like this exists to keep you from doing the one thing that protects you, which is stopping to think. Nothing in a real reading expires in an hour.

**Secrecy.** A reader who asks you to keep the work from your family or friends is protecting the arrangement, not you. Talking a reading over with someone you trust is healthy, and any reader worth booking will say so.

**Requests for more than the price.** Bank details, cash for a ritual, items to be "cleansed" and returned later. None of this is part of a reading. You pay the listed price through the checkout and that is the whole transaction.

If any of these show up, you don't owe anyone an explanation. Stop paying, keep your records, and if money has already changed hands, your state attorney general's consumer-protection office is the place to report it.

## What should you ask a tarot reader before booking?

Five questions cover most of it. A sound reader answers them plainly and doesn't hurry you. I have put my own answers after each, as a baseline.

1. **What exactly do I get, and when?** The format, the length, the delivery and the timeline. For me, a recorded reading is a video walkthrough plus a written synthesis within three business days, and a live reading is a Zoom session with a written synthesis afterward.
2. **What does it cost in total, and is there anything after?** You want one number, and the honest answer to the second half is usually "nothing, unless you book again." Mine are the listed prices, and checkout is the whole bill.
3. **Who actually does the reading?** On some services you choose from a roster; on a solo practice it should be the person whose name is on the site. On this site, it is me, start to finish.
4. **How do you work, and do you predict the future?** You are listening for a method you can understand. I read the cards as a structured way to look at a question you already have. I am not a psychic or a medium, and I don't claim to read minds or contact anyone.
5. **What won't you read on?** A good reader has an answer ready. Mine: a reading is not medical, legal or financial advice, and I won't present it as one.

You don't need to send all five. If the site already answers them, the way mine tries to on the [FAQ](/faq), you have most of what you need.

## What should a first reading feel like?

Grounded, and a little clarifying. You should come away with a clearer view of your situation and a few concrete things you might try, not a sense of doom and not a sales pitch.

A good reader takes your question seriously and may help you sharpen it, because the question shapes the reading more than people expect. [Asking a question the cards can answer](/guides/asking-a-question-the-cards-can-answer) covers how to frame one. During the reading, you should be able to follow the reasoning: this card, in this position, says this about your question. Jargon is fine if it gets explained.

It is fine for a reading to tell you something you didn't want to hear. That is often where it earns its keep. It is not fine for it to end with a bill for fixing what it found. You should not feel flattered into rebooking or frightened into it. If you book again, it should be because the first one was useful.

## Does the format change what to look for?

A little. For a recorded reading, the stated turnaround and what arrives matter most, since you won't be in the room. Check how the reader collects your question and whether they will ask if something is unclear. I take the question at checkout, in a sentence, and ask by email before I read if anything needs clarifying. [What a recorded tarot reading is](/guides/what-is-a-recorded-tarot-reading) explains the format in full.

For a live reading, check how scheduling works, where you will meet, and whether the length is fixed before you start, so the session can't quietly run on. [Recorded, live, or written](/guides/recorded-live-or-written-tarot-reading) compares the formats if you are still deciding between them.

## So how do you choose?

Start with the checks above. Once a reader passes them, what is left is fit: whether their way of reading suits how you think. Read a few of their guides or watch a clip, and see whether the reasoning makes sense to you.
